Evaluation of the course

You can work in small group (2 or 3 persons) but the report must be individual
The deadline to submit your mini-project/project is April, 30th 2026
send it to ensae.nlp@gmail.com with your Lab advisor in CC.
The grading criteria for project evaluation are:
Adherence to guidelines and report structure, quality of writing (no LLM style), delay : 3 points
Relevance of data analysis: 3 points
Releance of the state-of-the-art: 3 points
Relevance of the proposed models: 3 points
Implementation of the models: 3 points
Dicussion of results: 5 points
Assessment for this course will take the form of a personal project. The project consists in writing a scientific report on a chosen analysis of the Archelec corpus using NLP tools.

The Archelec corpus is a collection of documents consisting of the manifestos of candidates in the elections of the French fifth republic. These documents were collected and digitised by Sciences Po

Archelec, les archives électorales françaises de la Ve République, du papier au numérique : reflet fidèle ou distorsion ? Odile Gaultier-Voituriez.
More bibliographical references : Fonds Archives électorales

They have been imported and OCRed (transcribed) in Arkindex : https://demo.arkindex.org/browse/1bc39ca6-399b-47ca-9de1-ab2ef481cabb?top_level=true&folder=true

The project must consist of the following stages:

Define a problem on the corpus requiring the use of NLP techniques.
Retrieve, format and describe the data statistically
Search the literature on the chosen problem and write a short state-of-the-art
Propose and justify of the choice of the model(s)
Experiment with the proposed model
Write an analysis of the results and a conclusion
The article should follow the layout of the NEURIPS conference https://www.overleaf.com/latex/templates/neurips-2024/tpsbbrdqcmsh

Both the code and the PDF version of the article should be stored in a github repository.

How to get the data ?

The metadata can be downloaded in CSV format from the Archelec website : https://archelec.sciencespo.fr/explorer

The transcriptions are in a git repository: https://gitlab.teklia.com/ckermorvant/arkindex_archelec

Problem proposals (in increasing difficulty order)

You can choose a subject from the list below or propose your own (validate if with your Lab advisor)
Style analysis ⭐

Gender Classification: Is there a “feminine” or “masculine” rhetoric within the manifestos?
Political affiliation detection :
predict the titulaire-soutien (support) or liste (party list)
analyze confusion matrices to identify which parties “speak the same language”
compute the semantic similarity of the documents and display them in 2D with their political support or party list. Identify outliers.
Metadata extraction ⭐

Train a Named-entity extraction model using the metadata file.
Evaluate on the Archelec corpus
Evaluate on the more recent corpus https://github.com/regardscitoyens/professions-foi-candidats/tree/master
Sponsorship and personality extraction: Use NER to extract the names of national leaders mentioned (“De Gaulle” “Mitterrand”). Who cites whom? Does a local candidate prefer to link themselves to a national figure or distance themselves?
Spatial analysis: extract mentioned locations to determine if the discourse is purely rooted locally or if it projects toward national/international stakes.
Linguistic analysis ⭐

Semantic Density and “Wooden Language” (Langue de Bois) : measure the ratio of “meaning-carrying” words to “filler” or “abstract” words. Cross-reference with titulaire-profession or titulaire-soutien
The populism of simplicity: Linguistic complexity trends over time or accross parties. Calculate readability scores for every document and plot the mean complexity against the date, the titulaire-soutien or the titulaire-profession.
Topic Modeling & Temporal Evolution ⭐⭐

Thematic propensity by party: for a specific election, calculate the distribution of themes for each political side.
Specialization by party : identify themes where a party is significantly over-represented compared to the national average for that election.
Semantic mapping : use Topic Modeling to extract dominant themes, then project the different candidates or parties into this thematic space and plot a map
Correlation between topic and metadata: cross-reference dominant topics with socio-cultural metadata like titulaire-profession or titulaire-age. (e.g., “Do candidates from agricultural backgrounds focus on local farming themes or do they strictly follow their party’s national talking points?”).
Thematic sentiment analysis: Move beyond identifying what is being said to how it is being said. If two parties mention “Europe,” one might use defensive/skeptical vocabulary while the other uses positive/integrationist vocabulary
Discourse versus economic reality: compare manifesto themes with real-world economic indicators using the departement-insee code. For example, is the frequency of the “Unemployment” topic higher in departments where the actual unemployment rate is higher?
Analyzing the iconography of political manifestos ⭐⭐⭐

Analyse and measure the evolution of the visual style in time or accross party.
photo-heavy vs. text-heavy
color palettes used
presence of national symbols or objects
The candidate portrait analysis : Is the candidate alone or in a crowd? Are they smiling? Are they wearing a suit or casual clothing? What is the background (an office, a factory, or a rural field)? Cross-reference these features with the titulaire-profession or titulaire-sexe.
Multimodal sentiment: image versus text, does the image always say the same thing as the text? Compare the sentiment/tone of the text with the visual emotion of the photographies.
